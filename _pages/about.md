**Xilong Cheng** is a third-year Master’s student at the School of Information and Communication Engineering, Communication University of China, advised by [**Yunxiao Qin**](https://qyxqyx.github.io/homepage/). He has a strong interest in streaming media narratives, cognitive psychology, and behavioral psychology. His research focuses on leveraging AI technologies to build more generalizable role-playing systems, aiming to construct highly realistic social simulation environments.

His current research interests focus on anthropomorphic generative agents, social simulation, human-inspired representation learning, and human–computer interaction (HCI), including but not limited to:
1.	**Role-playing agents**: Investigating the role-playing capabilities of large language models (LLMs) and developing general role-playing agent frameworks that can support research in social sciences and psychology. Specifically, this line of research focuses on: (1) systematic and quantitative evaluation of role-playing abilities in large language models; (2) role-playing agents with long-term memory, long-horizon reasoning, and continual self-evolution; (3) high-fidelity social simulation environments built upon role-playing agents.
2.	**Human-inspired Representation Learning**: Exploring representation learning methods inspired by human cognition and behavioral mechanisms. This work aims to learn temporally structured and embodied representations from first-person videos and multimodal sensory data, enabling applications of large language models (LLMs) in embodied intelligence and robotic systems.
3.	**Human–Computer Interaction (HCI)**: Exploring new paradigms of human–computer interaction built upon the aforementioned agent and representation learning technologies. This research investigates how generative agents and embodied systems can enable more natural, efficient, and interpretable human–AI collaboration, and how such systems can be deployed in real-world interactive scenarios.

Meanwhile, he also has a strong interest in reinforcement learning and continual learning techniques for large language models.
